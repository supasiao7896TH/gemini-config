Identity study · round 01

A(i)CODER

Three ways to take the original banner further. Same wordmark, same paw, same tricolour bar — treated three different ways so the choice is about where the mark has to survive, not about taste alone.

SOURCE banner artwork, supplied OWNER supasiao7896TH RENAMED from SOICODER USE dev persona · web app YEAR MARK 2025, retained

Two readings, one word

A is the owner. (i) makes it AI. The name says who writes the code and what writes it with him, and the brackets are the join — an aside inside a name, the way a thought interrupts a sentence.

Drawn, not typed

The wordmark is a 5 × 7 bitmap rendered as vector rectangles. No font dependency, no licence to track, and it stays exactly as crisp at 2400 px as it is at 24.

What the round decides

Pick one direction. The next round turns it into the full kit — icon set, clear-space rules, motion, and the app shell it has to live in.

Open question

The brackets are the one thing worth arguing about

The rename is right. A(i)CODER is true to the person and true to how the work actually gets made, and it beats SOICODER on both counts. The double reading is the good kind of clever — it survives being explained. The brackets are a separate question, and they are the part that will cost something later. Three versions, drawn at the same size:

What the brackets cost: they cannot be typed into a GitHub handle, an npm package, a domain, or a filename. In a URL they become %28 and %29. So the written name and the typed name split apart on day one — the logo says A(i)CODER while everything addressable says aicoder, and that gap never closes.

The middle option gets the same two readings out of colour instead of punctuation. One accent glyph, no punctuation to strip, and it stays typeable everywhere. It also costs nothing to change your mind later: the accent is a fill, not a character.

Honest read: take the middle one. But this is a name, not a measurement — if the brackets are what you picture when you picture the brand, keep them. Everything on this page is already built for A(i)CODER, so that choice is made and working.

D1

Neon Arcade

→ light README · web app header · social card

The supplied artwork, resolved. Spacing put on the same 8 px grid the letters are drawn on, the bloom rebuilt as a two-pass Gaussian so it survives export, and the paws re-cut so the pads read as pads at any size. Every glyph and paw is a separate tube on its own phase, so the sign strikes unevenly and never blinks in unison.

D2

CRT Night

→ terminal · dark README · PWA splash

Same lockup, moved to a phosphor screen. Bloom runs hotter, a pink ghost sits one pixel off-register the way a cheap CRT actually misconverges, and a 3 px scanline field holds the whole plate together. The ghost lives inside the same tube as its letter, so a dropout takes both — a ghost left glowing over a dead letter is the tell that gives away a fake.

D3

Street Sticker

→ avatar · favicon · PWA icon · merch

Glow is a luxury of large sizes. Below about 64 px it turns a mark into a smudge — so this direction trades it for flat colour, a die-cut keyline and a hard offset shadow, and stacks the wordmark to fit a square. The paw is promoted to lead. It is the only one of the three that is still legible as a 16 px favicon.

The question

What happens on a dark app surface

A plate is a rectangle of light. Drop one into a dark interface and the eye finds the box before it finds the logo — four hard edges and two hairlines all announcing themselves. The fix is not a better rectangle. It is to stop shipping the ground attached to the mark.

Left is the problem. Middle is the same mark with no ground at all, which is what belongs in a header, a README or a splash screen. Right is the glow ground: light that falls off into the surface with no straight edge anywhere, which is what neon does to the air around it.

One more rule worth stating plainly: D1 and D2 are not two logos. They are one logo with a light mode and a dark mode. “D1 on a dark background” is not a thing to be fixed — the answer is D2 bare. Pastel violet #B39DF3 does not carry enough contrast on #0D0B14, which is the whole reason D2 lifts the wordmark to #C4AFFF.
